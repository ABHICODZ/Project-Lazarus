It all started when I came across [Rea - Reverse engineer anything](https://rea.tools/) on GitHub's trending repositories of the week. As I went through the project, I wanted to understand what it all meant.

In simple words, reverse engineering is like an investigator trying to crack a case or a hunter tracking down its prey using scents, footprints, and other clues. Here, the ground changes to strings, network logs, binaries, and a whole lot of other things to follow around until you find what you're looking for.

But this got me thinking about something else.

The limitation of possibility vs. the limitation of understanding.

There is a rift between what we believe is possible and what we actually understand. We encounter a problem, try everything we know, hit a wall, and sometimes just turn back, assuming we've reached the limit of what's achievable.

But reverse engineering is different. You bounce back. You dig deeper, going all the way down to the binary if necessary. It's about that stubbornness to solve a problem unconventionally, irrespective of how difficult or inaccessible it might seem.

And this made me think about the persistence and determination of humans, the very will that took us to the Moon, gave us the ability to explore space, and gave us the power to destroy ourselves and others.

I'm no expert, nor am I even a beginner in this field of study. I'm still trying to understand and comprehend all this information. But exploring this subject made me reflect on how often we mistake the limitations of our understanding for the limitations of possibility.

Perhaps the real challenge isn't just overcoming limitations, but understanding whether they exist because something is genuinely impossible or because we haven't figured it out yet.

On the Thermodynamics of Irreversible Computation in Quantum Lattice Horizons

Edward Vance — Scriptorium of Applied Physics | MS-ID: 1961-QLH-88b
∗ ∗ ∗

When an irreversible computational state collapses along a quantum lattice boundary, information is not casually extinguished; it is translated into thermodynamic entropy through a non-unitary projection. Following Landauer’s principle[1], every deleted bit incurs a minimum energy tax paid directly to the surrounding thermal bath, establishing a profound correspondence between algorithmic information theory and black hole event horizons[2].

The compression of phase space volume in any dissipative state register forces an irreducible divergence in local microstates. Where discrete nodes interact along the horizon membrane, microscopic reversibility is formally preserved within the bulk wave packet, yet selectively quarantined from local observers through boundary entanglement. As computation advances, the observer’s subjective clock registers merely an irreversible arrow of dissipation, whereas the boundary retains the encrypted phase conjugate.

“To erase information is to alter the state of the universe; the loss of predictability within the automaton is precisely balanced by the increase of thermal chaos in its immediate surroundings.”

— Rolf Landauer, 1961

Consider then an automaton configured as an array of microscopic spin-half polarizations bounded by an unyielding perimeter. The irreversible logical reset of register cells induces an instantaneous non-equilibrium heat flux &vecJQ directed into the manifold reservoir. At critical coupling thresholds, the total boundary entropy generation must reflect both computational bit-annihilation and surface flux integrals.

Equation (2.4) confirms that computational dissipation is fundamentally geometric. When discrete cellular matrices purge memory records, their informational debris radiates outward toward the boundary surface ∂Ω. Consequently, the erasure latency does not scale strictly with internal gate speed, but rather is constrained by the thermal conductance of the surrounding horizon vacuum.

In this framework, physical information functions identically to conserved charge. The apparent destruction of state vectors is shown to be an artifact of coarse-graining across trans-Planckian degrees of freedom. What early cyberneticians termed “lost computational states” are in truth fully transcribed into the boundary’s phase fluctuations, persisting indefinitely across cosmic horizon scales.
